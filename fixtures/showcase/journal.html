Artificial Intelligence in Anesthesiology: Current Techniques, Clinical Applications, and Limitations

Christopher W. Connor

Volume 131 · Issue 6 · doi:10.1097/ALN.0000000000002960

Abstract

Artificial intelligence has been advancing in fields including anesthesiology. This scoping review of the intersection of artificial intelligence and anesthesiology research summarizes the current state of the field, and identifies the methodological limitations that recur across studies published to date.

Introduction

A supervised learning model trained on retrospective single-center data may achieve excellent discrimination in internal validation and still fail on a neighboring institution's patients, because the shift in case mix is not represented anywhere in the training distribution.

Deep neural networks are particularly susceptible to this failure mode: their capacity is large enough to absorb site-specific artifacts as if those artifacts were physiology. External validation is therefore not an optional courtesy to the reader but the only evidence that a model has learned something transferable.
